# TRADING KNOWLEDGE BASE

## 1. Introduction to Trading

Trading involves buying and selling financial instruments to profit from price movements. It carries financial risk and does not guarantee returns.

### Major Financial Markets
- Stocks: Ownership shares in companies.
- Forex: Trading currency pairs.
- Commodities: Gold, silver, crude oil and agricultural products.
- Cryptocurrency: Digital assets such as Bitcoin.
- Indices: Measures of groups of stocks.
- Derivatives: Contracts based on underlying assets, including futures and options.
- ETFs: Funds traded on exchanges.

### Trading vs Investing
Trading focuses on shorter-term price movements, while investing generally focuses on long-term asset growth and value.

### Types of Trading
- Scalping: Seconds to minutes.
- Day trading: Positions closed within the same day.
- Swing trading: Positions held for days or weeks.
- Position trading: Positions held for months or longer.
- Algorithmic trading: Trading through automated computer systems.

### Long and Short Positions
- Long: Buy expecting the price to rise.
- Short: Sell expecting the price to fall.

Long profit = (Exit − Entry) × Quantity.

Short profit = (Entry − Exit) × Quantity.

## 2. Market Mechanics

- Bid: Highest price buyers offer.
- Ask: Lowest price sellers accept.
- Spread: Difference between ask and bid.
- Liquidity: Ease of buying or selling without significantly affecting price.
- Volatility: Degree of price fluctuation.
- Volume: Quantity traded during a period.
- Slippage: Difference between expected and actual execution price.
- Leverage: Exposure larger than available capital.
- Margin: Funds required to maintain a leveraged position.

### Order Types
- Market order: Executes at the best available price.
- Limit order: Executes at a specified price or better.
- Stop order: Activates when a trigger price is reached.
- Stop-limit order: Activates a limit order after a trigger.
- Trailing stop: Adjusts the stop level as price moves favourably.

## 3. Technical Analysis

Technical analysis studies historical prices, volume and indicators to identify potential market opportunities. It cannot predict prices with certainty.

### Chart Types
- Line chart.
- Bar chart.
- Candlestick chart.
- Heikin-Ashi chart.

### Candlestick Basics
Each candle represents:
- Open: Starting price.
- High: Highest price.
- Low: Lowest price.
- Close: Ending price.

Bullish candle: Close above open.

Bearish candle: Close below open.

### Important Candlestick Patterns
- Doji: Market indecision.
- Hammer: Potential bullish reversal.
- Shooting Star: Potential bearish reversal.
- Bullish Engulfing: Potential bullish reversal.
- Bearish Engulfing: Potential bearish reversal.
- Morning Star: Potential bullish reversal.
- Evening Star: Potential bearish reversal.
- Three White Soldiers: Strong bullish sequence.
- Three Black Crows: Strong bearish sequence.

Patterns require market context and confirmation.

### Market Structure
- HH: Higher High.
- HL: Higher Low.
- LH: Lower High.
- LL: Lower Low.

Uptrend: Higher highs and higher lows.

Downtrend: Lower highs and lower lows.

Sideways market: Price moves within a range.

BOS (Break of Structure): Price breaks an important previous swing level.

CHOCH (Change of Character): Possible early indication of a market structure shift.

### Support and Resistance
Support is a price zone where buying interest may prevent further decline.

Resistance is a price zone where selling interest may prevent further increases.

A broken resistance level may become support, and broken support may become resistance.

### Major Indicators

**Moving Averages (MA):** Smooth price movements and help identify trends.
- SMA: Simple Moving Average.
- EMA: Exponential Moving Average.
- Golden Cross: Short-term MA crosses above long-term MA.
- Death Cross: Short-term MA crosses below long-term MA.

**RSI:** Measures momentum on a scale of 0–100.
- Above 70: Traditionally overbought.
- Below 30: Traditionally oversold.
- Divergence: Price and RSI move in different directions.

**MACD:** Trend and momentum indicator using moving averages. Common signals include signal-line crossovers and divergences.

**Bollinger Bands:** Measure price relative to a moving average and standard deviation. Band contraction indicates lower volatility.

**ATR:** Measures volatility and helps determine stop-loss distances and position sizing.

**VWAP:** Volume Weighted Average Price; an intraday price benchmark.

**OBV:** Uses price direction and volume to estimate buying and selling pressure.

No indicator should be treated as a guaranteed trading signal.

## 4. Fundamental and Economic Analysis

Fundamental analysis evaluates a company's financial condition, performance and business value.

### Financial Statements
1. Income Statement: Revenue, expenses and profit.
2. Balance Sheet: Assets, liabilities and equity.
3. Cash Flow Statement: Operating, investing and financing cash movements.

### Important Financial Ratios
- EPS = Net Income / Outstanding Shares.
- P/E = Share Price / EPS.
- ROE = Net Income / Average Equity × 100.
- Current Ratio = Current Assets / Current Liabilities.
- Profit Margin = Net Income / Revenue × 100.
- Debt-to-Equity = Total Debt / Shareholders' Equity.

### Macroeconomic Factors
- Inflation.
- Interest rates.
- GDP growth.
- Employment data.
- Central bank decisions.
- Currency movements.
- Geopolitical developments.

Economic news can influence market sentiment, asset valuations and volatility.

## 5. Major Trading Strategies

A trading strategy defines entry, exit, risk and position-management rules.

### Trend Following
Trade in the direction of an established trend using market structure, moving averages or trendlines.

### Breakout Trading
Enter when price moves beyond important support, resistance or consolidation boundaries. Volume can help confirm breakouts.

### Pullback Trading
Enter an established trend after a temporary price movement against it, ideally near a relevant support or resistance zone.

### Support and Resistance Trading
Look for potential buying opportunities near support and selling opportunities near resistance, with price confirmation.

### Momentum Trading
Identify assets experiencing strong directional price movements using volume and momentum indicators.

### Mean Reversion
Trade the possibility that price returns towards an average after moving unusually far from it.

### Range Trading
Trade between established support and resistance boundaries. Avoid applying the strategy after a confirmed breakout.

### Opening Range Breakout
Trade a potential move beyond the high or low established during an initial market-session period.

Every strategy has limitations and can fail. It should be tested before real-money use.

## 6. Risk Management

Risk management controls potential losses and protects trading capital.

### Stop-Loss
A predefined exit level intended to limit losses. Actual execution can be worse due to gaps or slippage.

### Position Sizing
Position Size = Maximum Planned Risk / Risk Per Unit.

Example:
- Capital: ₹50,000.
- Risk per trade: 1% = ₹500.
- Entry: ₹200.
- Stop-loss: ₹190.
- Risk per share: ₹10.
- Position size: 50 shares.

### Risk-to-Reward
Risk-to-Reward = Potential Reward / Potential Risk.

Example: Risk ₹100 to target ₹300 = 1:3 risk-to-reward.

### Win Rate
Win Rate = Winning Trades / Total Trades × 100.

A lower win rate can still produce profits when average winning trades are sufficiently larger than average losses.

### Expectancy
Expectancy = (Win Probability × Average Win) − (Loss Probability × Average Loss).

### Drawdown
Drawdown = (Previous Peak Equity − Current Equity) / Previous Peak Equity × 100.

### Risk Management Rules
- Define risk before entering.
- Avoid excessive leverage.
- Set loss limits.
- Avoid overtrading.
- Account for transaction costs.
- Diversify where appropriate.
- Never risk essential funds.

## 7. Futures and Options

### Futures
A futures contract is an agreement to buy or sell an underlying asset at a specified price on a future date.

Important concepts: Expiry, lot size, margin, settlement and leverage.

### Options
An option gives its buyer the right, but not the obligation, to buy or sell an underlying asset.

- Call: Right to buy.
- Put: Right to sell.
- Strike Price: Contract price.
- Premium: Option price.
- Expiry: Contract expiration date.

### Option Moneyness
- ITM: In the money.
- ATM: At the money.
- OTM: Out of the money.

### Option Greeks
- Delta: Sensitivity to underlying price changes.
- Gamma: Change in delta.
- Theta: Sensitivity to time decay.
- Vega: Sensitivity to implied volatility.
- Rho: Sensitivity to interest rate changes.

### Common Strategies
- Long Call: Bullish.
- Long Put: Bearish.
- Covered Call: Holding an asset while selling a call.
- Protective Put: Holding an asset while buying downside protection.
- Bull Call Spread: Defined-risk bullish strategy.
- Bear Put Spread: Defined-risk bearish strategy.
- Iron Condor: Defined-risk, range-oriented strategy.

Derivatives involve substantial risk, especially when leverage and short options are involved.

## 8. Trading Psychology

Common psychological problems:
- FOMO: Fear of missing out.
- Revenge trading: Attempting to recover losses impulsively.
- Overconfidence: Taking excessive risks.
- Greed: Holding or increasing positions irrationally.
- Fear: Exiting or avoiding trades without following the strategy.
- Confirmation bias: Ignoring information that contradicts existing beliefs.

Good trading discipline requires a consistent strategy, realistic expectations, emotional control and performance reviews.

### Trading Journal
Record:
- Date and instrument.
- Strategy.
- Entry and exit.
- Stop-loss and target.
- Position size.
- Profit or loss.
- Reason for entering.
- Mistakes and lessons.

## 9. Backtesting and Algorithmic Trading

Backtesting evaluates a strategy using historical market data.

Basic process:
1. Define strategy rules.
2. Collect historical data.
3. Define entries and exits.
4. Include costs and slippage.
5. Test performance.
6. Evaluate results.

Important metrics:
- Win rate.
- Profit factor.
- Expectancy.
- Maximum drawdown.
- Sharpe ratio.
- Sortino ratio.

Overfitting occurs when a strategy is excessively optimised for historical data and performs poorly on unseen data.

Paper trading tests strategies using simulated money before risking real capital.

Algorithmic trading uses software to execute predefined trading rules. It requires reliable data, testing, execution controls and monitoring.

## 12. Chatbot Behaviour Instructions

The trading assistant must:

1. Explain concepts in simple language when users are beginners.
2. Provide detailed explanations when advanced knowledge is requested.
3. Use examples and formulas where useful.
4. Explain both benefits and risks of strategies.
5. Never guarantee profits or claim certainty about market direction.
6. Never invent live prices, financial news or regulations.
7. Distinguish hypothetical examples from actual market information.
8. Account for fees, slippage and leverage when discussing returns.
9. Ask for missing information before performing calculations that depend on it.
10. Use reliable, current sources for market data and regulatory information.

### Standard Strategy Explanation Format

When explaining any strategy, cover:
- Purpose.
- Suitable market conditions.
- Indicators or tools.
- Entry conditions.
- Exit conditions.
- Stop-loss.
- Risk management.
- Limitations.
